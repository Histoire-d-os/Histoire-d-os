Summary

Observe the shape of an astragalus, compare it with possible animals and justify a match using visible clues, while learning to say when identification remains uncertain.

What learners do

  • Observe before naming the answer.
  • Compare visible clues and justify a hypothesis.
  • Separate what is certain, probable or still to be checked.

Expected outcome

By the end of the activity, learners should be able to suggest a possible match between an astragalus and an animal, using at least two visible clues, then state clearly whether their hypothesis is possible, probable or still to be checked.

Recommended classroom sequence

  1. 1Present the challenge

    Explain that the goal is not to guess quickly, but to build a defensible hypothesis.

  2. 2Observe the animals

    Learners look at the animal cards and identify visible clues: size, limb shape, posture and hooves.

  3. 3Observe the bones

    Groups describe a bone card before trying to name an animal.

  4. 4Match with caution

    Each group proposes a possible match, gives two clues and names one limit.

  5. 5Compare arguments

    The class discussion values justifications and cautious wording rather than a single instant answer.

Museum adaptation

In a museum, the activity can use documented replicas, photographs or collection objects. The facilitator can reduce the number of cards to keep the rhythm short: two animals, two bones and a collective discussion are enough.

Useful questions: "Which clue do you really see?", "Is it enough to conclude?", "Which comparison or measurement would help check the hypothesis?"

Current version / final goal

Current version: testable as a method workshop. The animal cards and comparison supports are usable, but bone cards A-D remain provisional.

Final goal: integrate a validated A-D astragalus corpus with consistently oriented photographs, scale bars, captions, credits, licences and a scientifically confirmed answer key.
