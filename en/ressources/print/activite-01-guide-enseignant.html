Find the astragalus

Goal

Help learners understand that a bone is located through clues: position, articulation, comparison and role in movement. The aim is not to train specialists in identification, but to practise observing, forming a hypothesis and justifying it.

Practical notes

Age

8-12 years.

Duration

30 to 45 min in a museum, 45 to 60 min in class.

Material

Learner sheet, image support, pencils, optional projection.

Vocabulary to introduce

Talus is the usual modern anatomical term. Astragalus is used in the project and in many historical or archaeological contexts. You can tell learners that both words refer here to the same bone, while keeping the nuance.

Quick preparation

  • Print the learner sheet or prepare a projection of the image support.
  • Keep the answer key for the group discussion, not for the start of the activity.
  • Prepare minimal vocabulary: foot, tarsus, talus/astragalus, joint, clue.
  • Use a short instruction: "Describe first, search afterwards, justify with two clues."

Museum adaptation

Use the activity as a short station in front of a display case, photograph, skeleton or replica. Ask visitors to locate a region of the limb rather than an isolated bone. No heritage object should be handled without authorisation.

Quick assessment

  • Successful: the learner locates the tarsal region and gives two clues.
  • Partly successful: the learner locates a plausible area but gives little justification.
  • To revisit: the learner guesses or confuses the astragalus with the visible heel without justification.

Differentiation and prompts

  • Support: ask only for the region between leg and foot.
  • Extension: ask learners to compare human, sheep and cow.
  • Prompt: "Which detail makes you think that?"
  • Prompt: "What would you need to be sure?"
