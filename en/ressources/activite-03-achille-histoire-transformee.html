Achilles: a changing story

Compare images and stories to understand how a tradition changes.

Summary

Discover that the story of Achilles has not always been told in the same way, distinguish ancient narrative, later tradition and modern images, then understand why sources must be checked before interpreting an image.

What learners do

  • Observe before naming the answer.
  • Compare visible clues and justify a hypothesis.
  • Separate what is certain, probable or still to be checked.

Expected outcome

By the end of the activity, learners should be able to distinguish an ancient narrative, a later tradition and a modern image. They should also explain that a modern image can represent a tradition without directly proving what the earliest stories said.

Recommended classroom sequence

  1. 1Start from the expression

    Ask what "Achilles' heel" means today and write down initial ideas.

  2. 2Read or tell the story

    Use the opening story as a teaching support, without confusing it with an ancient source.

  3. 3Sort the documents

    Learners classify cards or images into three categories: ancient narrative, later tradition, modern image.

  4. 4Question the images

    For each image, ask who made it, when, and what it actually shows.

  5. 5Write a conclusion

    Learners write one sentence explaining that the story known today results from successive transformations.

Museum adaptation

In a museum, the activity can become a short discussion around an image, artwork or object linked to Achilles. The facilitator does not need to unfold the whole chronology: comparing two contrasting documents, such as a modern work and an older source or label, is enough.

Useful questions: "Is this an ancient source or a later representation?", "What does the image show?", "What can it not prove on its own?"

Current version / final goal

Current version: testable with publishable images and cautious wording. It already supports work on the distinction between source, tradition and representation.

Final goal: complete the corpus with a few validated source cards, adapted for children, to distinguish what ancient narratives say, what later traditions add and what modern images represent.
