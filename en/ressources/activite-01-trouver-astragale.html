Find the astragalus

Look for a foot bone in humans and in different animals.

Summary

Locate the talus/astragalus in the foot, understand its place in the tarsus and recognise that a comparable bone also exists in other mammals, even when their feet do not look like ours.

What learners do

  • Observe before naming the answer.
  • Compare visible clues and justify a hypothesis.
  • Separate what is certain, probable or still to be checked.

Expected outcome

By the end of the activity, learners should be able to point to the tarsal region where the talus/astragalus is located, explain why it is not simply the visible heel, and give two clues to justify its location in humans and in comparison with another mammal.

Recommended classroom sequence

  1. 1Launch the investigation

    Show the isolated bone and ask: "Where could this bone be hidden in the foot? Why?" Do not give the answer immediately.

  2. 2Describe before answering

    Learners note what they see: general shape, hollows, bumps, surfaces and possible orientation.

  3. 3Locate

    They circle a probable region of the foot or limb and write at least one clue.

  4. 4Compare

    They transfer their landmark to the foot or skeleton of another mammal, for example a sheep.

  5. 5Conclude carefully

    Each group writes a sentence with two clues and says what would still need checking with a modern diagram or a real skeleton.

Museum adaptation

In a museum, the activity can work as a short station in front of a display case, photograph, skeleton or replica. The facilitator can ask visitors to look for a region of the limb rather than an isolated bone. The key instruction is to justify answers with visible clues, without handling any heritage object unless the museum rules allow it.

Useful questions: "Which part of the limb are you looking at?", "Which clues point to this area?", "What else would you need to see to be more certain?"
