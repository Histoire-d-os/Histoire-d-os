Facilitator guide
One bone, many lives

Guide to conducting an investigation into the possible uses of an object without announcing a definitive function.

1. In brief

The students describe an object without attributing a use to it, formulate at least two hypotheses, choose the clues that support them then list the information necessary to decide between them. Cards A to E are templates: the activity becomes fully usable after adding a validated corpus.

2. Objectives

  • Distinguish observation, hypothesis, interpretation and verification.
  • Describe material, shape, dimensions and visible traces.
  • Propose several possible uses from clues.
  • Understand the role of the archaeological and documentary context.

3. Expected success

Successful

Two hypotheses are linked to clues and to means of verification.

In progress

The hypotheses are distinct, but the clues remain imprecise.

To resume

The function is announced as certain from the first observation.

4. Duration and material

ContextDurationMaterial
Class45-75 minsSheet, A-E cards, photographs or validated replicas.

5. Quick preparation

Select one to five documented objects. Complete the cards without writing a function as a title. Keep context data hidden until the verification phase. Adapt the protocol to the rules of the institution.

“Start with what is visible. A hypothesis is a proposition to be tested, not a certain answer. »

6. Detailed procedure

7. Questions and reminders

Observe

  • What can you measure?
  • What traces are visible?
  • What does this view not show?

Check

  • What context would decide between the hypotheses?
  • Which reference object to compare to?
  • What analysis would be useful?

8. Responses or expected elements

No A-E function is expected before integrating the corpus. A successful production contains an independent description, at least two hypotheses, the associated clues, an objection or limit and a means of verification: context of discovery, traces of use, typological comparison, analysis of material, measurement or textual source.

9. Differentiation

NeedAdaptation
HelpWork on a single object and provide vocabulary labels.
Lighten the writingClassify “I observe/I suppose/I verify” cards.
Go deeperCompare two records and discuss what changes with provenance.

10. Museum adaptation

Choose an object adapted to the institution and its actual collections. Observation can be done in the window; a photograph or replica is a possible variation but does not entirely replace the original. Manipulation is only considered if the object can be handled, if the museum rules authorize it and according to the conservation protocol.

11. Quick assessment

Highlight in a conclusion an observation, a hypothesis and a means of verification. Evaluate the ability to change your mind when the context brings a new clue.

12. Common mistakes

Do not assert that a knucklebone was necessarily used for playing, predicting or counting. Do not announce an undocumented museum validation. Do not present a modern digital adaptation as a historical reconstruction.

  • Name the object before describing it.
  • Keep only one hypothesis.
  • Confusing resemblance and same usage.
  • Ignore discovery context.

13. Sources and limits

The support transposes an archaeological investigation approach: observation, formulation of hypotheses, comparison with the context and comparison. The five Metropolitan Museum of Art images documented as provisional in the redesign are excluded from the PDFs. The coding game is a contemporary extension, not proof of ancient use.

14. Current status and remaining validations

Templates and method ready for adult testing. Before a student session, integrate documented A-E objects, check notices and rights, establish the information revealed at each stage and have the corpus validated by scientific and museum managers.

Useful reminders

When an idea comes too quickly

“What exactly did you see?” Can you write the same sentence without naming the function? »

When the group gets stuck

“What other story would explain the same trace? What information would allow us to choose? »

What the correction does not contain

There is no “A = game, B = divination…” table: the objects have not yet been selected or validated. Even with a definitive corpus, certain functions may remain debated. The answer key should present the clues, sources and degree of confidence rather than a simplified answer.

Digital extension

The game “Coding with the knucklebone” can extend the discussion on contemporary technical use. It must be presented as a modern educational creation, not as the reconstruction of a documented ancient practice.

Complete this correction only after validation of the objects, notices, rights and interpretations by the scientific and museum managers.

Bone Stories - Activity 4

Sources, credits and limits

Keep with the complete kit.

Method

This kit organizes a general educational approach to investigation: describe, question, propose several hypotheses, compare the context and conclude according to the degree of confidence. The precise archaeological formulations will depend on the future corpus.

Media excluded

The five Metropolitan Museum of Art tentative photographs associated with locations A through E in the redesign are not published in these PDFs. Their notices, their educational relevance and the chain of rights must still be validated.

digital game

“Coding with the ossicle” is a contemporary interactive extension. It constitutes neither an archaeological source, nor proof of ancient use, nor a validated reconstruction.

Status

Method templates for adult preparation and testing. Objects A-E, notices, sources, credits, interpretations and physical printing remain to be completed or validated.

Working translation: specialist scientific, educational and legal wording requires human review.
